Quick answer

Start with a topic booster if your child needs paper practice, works best offline, is in Stage 7 or 8, or has a parent with little time to sit nearby. Start with CoreMark Coach if your child is in Cambridge Lower Secondary Stage 9 and needs teaching, not just more questions. Coach costs ₹9,999 per stage per academic year, as of 20 October 2026.

Who should buy only the boosters

Boosters alone are enough for many families. If your child already understands a topic and mainly needs more exam-style questions on it, a topic booster is a complete piece of practice for that topic, and it starts at ₹249.

Choose boosters only, without Coach, if one or more of these describe your child:

  • Your child can explain the topic but loses marks on questions.
  • Your child is in Stage 7 or 8.
  • You want paper practice that can be printed and done at a desk.
  • Your home internet is unreliable.
  • You do not want a parent to set up an account or sit in on sessions.

You can try before you decide. The free Stage 8 Algebra booster shows the format of a booster from start to finish. If you are weighing paper practice against full past papers, our guide to Checkpoint past papers and topic boosters covers the trade-off in more detail.

Who should buy only Coach

Coach is the better first purchase when a Stage 9 child needs to be taught, not only tested. It works through one sub-concept at a time, gives an everyday example, and then asks the child to explain the idea in their own words. If the explanation is vague, Coach re-teaches the idea with a different example rather than moving on.

Buy Coach alone only if all of these are true:

  • Your child is in Cambridge Lower Secondary Stage 9, because Coach is available for Stage 9 only for now.
  • A parent is happy to use their own Claude account, since Claude accounts are for adults, and to check in during the first few sessions.
  • Your child will sit at a computer or laptop with internet for each session.
  • You are prepared to pay the Coach price for a whole stage, not for one topic.

Coach is not for every Stage 9 family. If your child mainly needs paper practice on two or three topics, a booster does that for much less. Coach runs on the free Claude plan, and Claude Pro helps for long sessions, but Pro is not required. You can read more about the set-up on the Coach page.

How to use both in one week

If you buy both, give each one a different job. Coach does the teaching and the checking of understanding. The booster does the paper. The plan below uses one topic, and you can repeat it for the next topic.

  1. Monday. Open a fresh Coach chat, say "learn" and the topic, and work through the sub-concepts Coach lists.
  2. Tuesday. Ask Coach for a short exam on the same topic. Show your working on every question, and let Coach mark the paper once it has all your answers.
  3. Wednesday. A lighter day. Coach brings weak topics back for a quick review when they come up, so there is no need to force a session.
  4. Thursday. Print the booster for the same topic and have your child sit it as paper practice, with Coach closed.
  5. Friday. Mark the booster against its solutions. Write down each question that cost marks, and bring those questions to Coach in your next chat.

Start a fresh chat for each session, because shorter chats are easier to look back through. Keep Memory switched on in Claude settings, and if Coach seems to have lost track, tell it where you left off. Memory is a Claude feature, so CoreMark cannot guarantee that it saves every conversation.

What Coach keeps from the boosters, and where it differs

Coach was built as an extension of the boosters, so the habits carry over. It uses the same summary-sheet rules, the same marking style and the same common-mistake notes. A child who has already used a booster will recognise the way Coach presents a topic and explains a mistake.

The biggest practical difference is the ending. A booster stops at 20 questions, and once they are done, the child needs another booster. Coach keeps producing fresh questions for as long as the session lasts, within what your Claude plan allows. The trade-off is that a booster works without a screen, while Coach does not.

Coach's marks are practice marks. They are not Cambridge Checkpoint scores, and the way Checkpoint is scored is explained in our grading system guide. When an answer looks wrong, ask Coach to show its working, and check the step against the booster solution where you have one.

What ₹9,999 buys compared with topic boosters

Coach costs ₹9,999 per stage per academic year, paid once, with no subscription and no auto-renewal. The early bird price is ₹4,999 for the first 100 families. The Stage 9 edition works until 30 June 2027, and then Coach signs off for the year. These prices are as of 20 October 2026.

Topic boosters start at ₹249 each. To compare, count the topics your child needs help with this year and multiply by the booster price. A family buying boosters for three or four weak topics may spend well below the Coach price. A family that wants teaching across Maths, Science and Computing for the whole stage is paying for something different, because a booster covers one topic and Coach covers the stage.

Coach is a paid product and it does not replace the paper practice a booster gives. If the money is tight, buy the booster for the topic that is hurting the most, and revisit Coach later.
