Cambridge Checkpoint results 2026: when they come out, and how to read them

Two Checkpoint test series run in 2026. Cambridge releases results to schools on fixed dates, but your school controls when you hear. Here is what to expect and how to make sense of the result.

SP
Snehal Patel • Published September 25, 2026 • 5 min read • Based on Cambridge official key dates
Quick answer

Cambridge runs two Checkpoint test series in 2026: March (tests 2-12 March) and May (tests 11-21 May). Results are released to schools in May and July, not directly to parents. Your school then shares the result with you — timing varies by school. When you receive the report, skip the overall score and go straight to the strand-level subscores, which show exactly which topics to target in revision.

2026 Checkpoint test series and result dates

Cambridge releases Checkpoint results to schools on two different schedules depending on which series your child sits.

Series Test Dates Feedback/Results to Schools Full Reports Available
March 2026 2–12 March Diagnostic feedback: 7 May End-of-series reports: Mid-May
May 2026 11–21 May Results and moderator reports: 23 July End-of-series reports: Early August

Important: these dates are for schools, not parents

Cambridge releases results to your child's school on the dates shown above via their internal portal (called Direct). Your school then decides when to share the result with parents. Some schools pass results on the same day; others may take 1-2 weeks. If you're waiting for your result, ask your school when they plan to release it — they will know the internal timeline.

Understanding your Checkpoint result

When the result arrives, the report includes four key pieces of information: an overall 0-50 score, a performance band (Unclassified, Basic, Aspiring, Good, High, or Outstanding), a set of strand-level subscores, and diagnostic feedback from the examiner. The strand-level subscores are the most useful — they tell you which topics to prioritise in revision. For a full breakdown of what each band means, what counts as a good score, and how to read the band system, see our guide to the Cambridge Checkpoint grading system.

Ask for the strand breakdownThe result sheet your school gives you should include strand-level subscores. If it only shows the overall score, ask the school for the Statement of Results, which includes the full breakdown. Cambridge provides it to schools, and it should be shared with parents.

The Checkpoint results report includes: (1) an overall subject score on a 0-50 scale, (2) a performance band (Unclassified, Basic, Aspiring, Good, High, or Outstanding) based on that score, (3) strand-level subscores for each curriculum area (e.g., Algebra, Geometry, and Statistics in Maths), and (4) diagnostic feedback. A child with a score of 28 overall might be strong in Geometry but weak in Algebra; different strand profiles need different revision plans. Ask your school for the Statement of Results (which includes strand breakdown) if you only receive the overall score. Then identify the lowest-scoring strand and use that to guide revision focus rather than trying to revise the entire subject at once.
