IGCSE Checkpoint: what the Stage 9 test tells you about the IGCSE years

Schools and parents often call it "IGCSE Checkpoint". The name mixes up two different things. Here is what the test is, what it does not count for, and how to read the report so the first IGCSE year starts in the right place.

Quick answer

"IGCSE Checkpoint" usually means Cambridge Lower Secondary Checkpoint, the test many schools give at the end of Stage 9, around age 14. It is not part of IGCSE and gives no IGCSE grade. Cambridge marks it on a 0 to 50 scale per subject, and schools use the report to plan the two IGCSE years that follow.

Why it gets called "IGCSE Checkpoint"

Checkpoint is the last Cambridge test before IGCSE starts, and many schools describe their whole secondary route as "the IGCSE school". So the Stage 9 test picks up the IGCSE label. On Google, "igcse checkpoint" is a real search: it showed up 168 times in Google results for this site in the three months to 26 October 2026.

The official name is Cambridge Lower Secondary Checkpoint. Cambridge's own page puts it this way: "Many schools use our Cambridge Checkpoint tests to assess learners at the end of the lower secondary programme (Stage 9)" (Cambridge Lower Secondary Checkpoint). "Many schools" is the key phrase. It is the school's choice, not a requirement for IGCSE.

It is not a small test. Cambridge says it receives over 160,000 Lower Secondary Checkpoint entries a year, where one entry is one learner sitting one subject (Cambridge facts and figures). IGCSE itself is offered by over 7,000 schools in 147 countries, per the same page.

The details of the score itself, including why 30 out of 50 is not 60%, are in our guide to the Checkpoint grading system. For a side by side of the two programmes, see Cambridge Lower Secondary vs IGCSE.

Does a Checkpoint score predict IGCSE results?

Cambridge says yes, with a qualifier. In its December 2025 results release, it reported that its research found "Cambridge Lower Secondary Checkpoint scores are a good predictor of performance in Cambridge IGCSEs, particularly in the same or a related subject" (Cambridge, 18 Dec 2025). The same release says schools that adopted Checkpoint saw "a small but significant improvement in their average Cambridge IGCSE grade" compared with schools that did not.

Two honest caveats. This is Cambridge's own research on its own tests (the study is Tim Gill, "An analysis of the relationship between Secondary Checkpoint and IGCSE results", Cambridge University Press & Assessment, 2023). And "predictor" describes groups of students, not a promise about one child. Two years of teaching sit between the tests. The useful reading is narrower: the subject and strand where the score is weak is where IGCSE work is most likely to feel hard first.

From the Checkpoint Maths report to IGCSE Maths topics

The Checkpoint report gives marks by strand, not just one Maths number. Cambridge names the Lower Secondary Maths strands as Number, Algebra, Geometry and Measure, and Statistics and Probability (Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics). IGCSE Maths 0580 is organised into nine topic areas (0580 syllabus, 2025 to 2027). This table lines them up, so a weak strand on the report points to specific IGCSE chapters.

Checkpoint Maths strandIGCSE 0580 topic areas it feedsIf this strand was weak
Number1 NumberFix this first. Standard form, indices and rounding appear across every IGCSE paper, and one paper at each tier is non-calculator.
Algebra2 Algebra and graphs; 3 Coordinate geometryExpect the steepest jump. Rearranging formulae and solving equations come back in graphs and coordinate work.
Geometry and Measure4 Geometry; 5 Mensuration; 6 Trigonometry; 7 Transformations and vectorsFour IGCSE topic areas build on this one strand. Angle facts, area and volume need to be automatic before trigonometry.
Statistics and Probability8 Probability; 9 StatisticsUsually the easiest gap to close. Practise reading and drawing charts, and listing outcomes.

The grouping is CoreMark's, made by reading both syllabuses. Cambridge does not publish an official strand-to-topic map.

💡
Read across, not just downCompare your child's strand marks with each other before you compare them with anyone else. A strong Number mark with a weak Algebra mark tells you more about the IGCSE year than the overall score does.

Core or Extended: the IGCSE Maths decision after Stage 9

IGCSE Maths 0580 is tiered. The syllabus says the Core content is for learners targeting grades C to G, and the Extended content, which includes all of Core plus more, is for those targeting A* to C.

TierPapersEach paperGrades available
CorePaper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator)1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marksC to G
ExtendedPaper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator)2 hours, 100 marksA* to E

Source: Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027.

The tier matters because a Core candidate cannot get above a C. The school decides the entry. Ask the school two direct questions: does it use Checkpoint results when it sets Maths groups, and when is the tier decision final? A Checkpoint report with weak Algebra is a reason to work on Algebra early in Stage 10, not a verdict on the tier.

What to do with the report

  1. Find the strand marks. The learner report gives a score at subject level and marks at strand level. The school-level report also shows an international average for each subject and strand, so ask the school how your child's class compared.
  2. Pick one weak strand per subject. Use the table above to see which IGCSE topics it feeds.
  3. Work on it before IGCSE teaching reaches it, topic by topic, with questions that show working. CoreMark's Maths and Science boosters are built one Lower Secondary topic at a time for this.
  4. If the test is still ahead, start with where to find Checkpoint past papers and what the Checkpoint exam covers.

Frequently asked questions

"IGCSE Checkpoint" is the informal name for Cambridge Lower Secondary Checkpoint, a test many Cambridge schools give at the end of Stage 9, around age 14. It covers English, Maths and Science, is marked by Cambridge on a 0 to 50 scale per subject, and comes before IGCSE. It is not an IGCSE exam and gives no IGCSE grade.

Not by Cambridge. Cambridge says many schools use Checkpoint at the end of Stage 9, which makes it the school's choice rather than an entry requirement for IGCSE. A school can still set its own rules for moving into IGCSE classes, so ask your child's school what it uses.

No. Checkpoint and IGCSE are separate assessments, and no Checkpoint marks carry over. Cambridge's research does find that Checkpoint scores are a good predictor of IGCSE performance, especially in the same or a related subject, so a weak strand is worth fixing early in the IGCSE course rather than ignoring.
