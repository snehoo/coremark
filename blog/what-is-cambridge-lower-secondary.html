What is Cambridge Lower Secondary? The full picture, in one place.

Parents new to the Cambridge Pathway often hear "Stage 8" or "Checkpoint" without anyone explaining how the pieces fit. This page is the map — what Cambridge Lower Secondary is, its three stages and three subjects, how Checkpoint works, and what comes next.

SP
Snehal Patel • Published September 25, 2026 • 6 min read
Quick answer

Cambridge Lower Secondary is Cambridge International's curriculum framework for Stage 7, 8 and 9 — roughly ages 11 to 14. It covers Mathematics, Science and Computing, and ends with the Cambridge Checkpoint assessment. It is not a qualification itself — it's the stage between Cambridge Primary and Cambridge IGCSE.

What Cambridge Lower Secondary actually is

Cambridge Lower Secondary is one of four stages in the Cambridge Pathway offered by Cambridge Assessment International Education. It sits after Cambridge Primary and before Cambridge Upper Secondary (most commonly Cambridge IGCSE), covering three school years — Stage 7, 8 and 9.

It is a curriculum framework, not an exam board qualification: schools teach to Cambridge's published learning objectives for each subject and stage, and the framework is used by Cambridge-affiliated schools worldwide, including a large number of international schools in India. That international reach is why the programme is sometimes searched as "Cambridge International Lower Secondary" — it's the same framework, just described with the exam board's full name.

The three stages: 7, 8 and 9

Each stage corresponds to one school year, with content that builds on the one before it.

The three subjects

Cambridge Lower Secondary covers three subjects, each with its own Cambridge syllabus code and its own Checkpoint score.

SYLLABUS 0862
Mathematics
Number, Algebra, Geometry and Measure, Statistics and Probability.
 SYLLABUS 0893
Science
Biology, Chemistry and Physics, taught as one combined subject.
 SYLLABUS 0860
Computing
Computational thinking, data handling, networks and computer systems.

Cambridge Checkpoint: the assessment at the end

Cambridge Checkpoint is the assessment that closes out Lower Secondary, usually sat in Stage 9. It tests a narrower set of subjects than the curriculum itself — English, Mathematics and Science — each scored separately on a 0 to 50 scale, with results grouped into six performance bands from Unclassified to Outstanding, plus a strand-level breakdown. Computing is taught and assessed by the school itself, not through a Checkpoint paper.

Checkpoint is diagnostic, not pass/fail — schools use it to plan teaching and revision, not to hold students back. For what the Checkpoint experience actually involves, read what Cambridge Checkpoint is; for exactly how the scoring and bands work, see the full grading system guide.

What comes next: Cambridge IGCSE

After Stage 9 and Checkpoint, students move into Cambridge Upper Secondary — most commonly Cambridge IGCSE. Lower Secondary and IGCSE are separate, sequential programmes, not two names for the same thing: Lower Secondary builds the foundation, and IGCSE is where students begin subject-specific qualifications. For the full comparison, see how Cambridge Lower Secondary and IGCSE connect.
