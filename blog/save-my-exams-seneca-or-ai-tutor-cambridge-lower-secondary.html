Parents searching for a revision website for a Stage 7 to 9 child often land on the two best-known names in UK-style revision. Both are well regarded, and both were built for a different set of exams. The useful question is what kind of help your child needs and whether the site's course list includes the stage they are in.

What job does each of the three tools do?

Think of them as three different jobs. A notes-and-questions site gives you explanations to read and questions to attempt, usually organised by exam specification. A gamified practice platform turns revision into short tasks with progress and rewards. An AI tutor holds a conversation, asks your child to explain, and marks the answers they write.

Save My Exams sits in the first group and Seneca mostly in the second, with Seneca's higher plans adding AI-marked questions. Both also have free tiers. CoreMark Coach is in the third group, for one stage.

Do Save My Exams or Seneca cover Cambridge Lower Secondary?

We could not find Cambridge Lower Secondary, Checkpoint or Stage 9 content on either site. Save My Exams' navigation lists qualifications including GCSE, IGCSE, Cambridge (CIE), Edexcel, Oxford AQA, AQA, OCR and WJEC. Seneca's navigation lists GCSE, A Level, KS3, KS2, Primary, 11+, BTEC, International A Level and IB Diploma, and shows "iGCSE" course links.

Absence from a search is not proof of absence, so treat this as a prompt to check, not a verdict. Courses are added over time, and your child's school may also have access to material we cannot see.

⚠️
KS3 is not Stage 9 KS3 means Key Stage 3 in England, which covers roughly the same ages as Stages 7 to 9. The age band is similar. The curriculum, wording and assessment are different from Cambridge Lower Secondary.

What would a Cambridge Lower Secondary course need to match?

Cambridge publishes a curriculum framework for each subject, and the codes are the quickest way to check a resource: 0862 for Mathematics, 0893 for Science and 0860 for Computing. A resource built for Stages 7 to 9 should use those frameworks' strands and wording. Checkpoint, the end-of-stage assessment, is scored on a 0 to 50 scale, explained in our Checkpoint grading guide. If a site talks about grades 9 to 1, A* to G or percentage pass marks, it is describing a different qualification.

Which kind of mistake is your child making?

Choose the tool by the mistake, not by the brand. Look at three recent wrong answers and sort them.

What went wrongWhat usually helpsTool type
"I do not understand the idea"A different explanation, then the child explaining it backAI tutor or human tutor
"I understood it last week and forgot"Short, spaced questions on the same topicPractice platform or topic booster
"I know it but lost marks"Marking that shows method marks and wordingExam-style marking, tutor or AI
"I ran out of time"Timed papers on paperPrinted papers
"I panicked"Calm repetition and a person who reassuresHuman tutor, parent

Most children make more than one kind, so it is normal to need two tools. The point is to avoid paying for a notes site when the real problem is time pressure, or the other way round.

What does an AI tutor add that a notes site does not?

It answers the follow-up. A notes page explains a method once, and if your child still does not follow, the only option is to reread it. A tutor can try a different example, ask the child to explain it back, and tell them which step went wrong. Coach also marks method and accuracy marks across a full paper and groups the mistakes by idea.

The cost of that is that it can be wrong. A published revision note is written and edited once, so every reader sees the same text. An AI answer is generated fresh each time, which is why we suggest checking samples against a worked solution.

What do notes and practice sites do better?

They are stable, scannable and cheap. A child can read a clear page in two minutes, skim it again the night before, and use a free tier at no cost. Large question banks, past-paper style sets and structured courses are what these sites are built for, and many IGCSE students use them. If your child is heading to IGCSE next year, it makes sense to look at them then. The CoreMark vs Save My Exams post covers that handover in detail.

How can you check any revision site's coverage in three minutes?

  1. Search the site for "Lower Secondary", "Checkpoint" and "Stage 9". No results is a warning.
  2. Look for the syllabus code: 0862 for Maths, 0893 for Science, 0860 for Computing.
  3. Open one topic your child is studying now and compare the wording with their school notes.
  4. Check the age or level shown on the course. A different curriculum with a similar age band, such as KS3, is a different thing.
  5. Read the price page for the plan you would actually need, not the headline plan.

The same checks work on any new tool a friend recommends.

Which should you pick for your situation?

For a child who mainly needs printed, structured practice, boosters or the free tiers of general sites are sensible starting points, and our post on practice resources in India compares them. For a Stage 7, 8 or 9 child who needs explanation and marked practice, Coach is built for exactly that, at a price you should weigh against what you already pay. For a child about to start IGCSE, notes and question-bank sites such as Save My Exams become relevant. There is no single best choice, because the three tools solve different problems.
