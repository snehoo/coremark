Cambridge Primary Checkpoint grading, explained for parents

Cambridge Primary Checkpoint assesses students at the end of Stage 6 using a grading system identical to Lower Secondary — but what does it mean for your child's transition to Stage 7? Here's what the scores, bands and results actually tell you.

SP
Snehal Patel • Published September 25, 2026 • 6 min read • Reviewed against Cambridge Primary syllabus
Quick answer

Cambridge Primary Checkpoint results are scored on a 0–50 scale and described using six performance bands (Unclassified, Basic, Aspiring, Good, High, Outstanding). This system is identical to Lower Secondary Checkpoint. Three subjects are fully externally examined: English, Maths, and Science. Global Perspectives is a team project marked by teachers and moderated by Cambridge. A Primary result is a snapshot of your child's readiness for Lower Secondary Stage 7.

The Primary Checkpoint grading scale and bands

Cambridge Primary Checkpoint reports results for students at the end of Stage 6 using a 0 to 50 scaled score, placed into one of six performance bands. The same six bands apply regardless of subject or stage: Unclassified, Basic, Aspiring, Good, High, and Outstanding.

Feature Cambridge Primary Checkpoint Cambridge Lower Secondary Checkpoint
Stage & age Stage 6, age ~10–11 Stages 7–9, age 11–14
Subjects tested English (L1/L2), Maths, Science, Global Perspectives (team project) English, Maths, Science (no Global Perspectives)
Scoring scale 0–50 (via Rasch statistical analysis) 0–50 (via Rasch statistical analysis)
Performance bands Unclassified, Basic, Aspiring, Good, High, Outstanding Unclassified, Basic, Aspiring, Good, High, Outstanding

The 0–50 score reported on the Primary Checkpoint statement is a scaled score, not a percentage or raw mark count. Cambridge uses a statistical process called Rasch analysis to convert raw paper marks to this scale, which corrects for one test paper being slightly harder or easier than another — so a score of 30 stays comparable no matter which year a student sat the exam.

How Primary and Lower Secondary grading relate

The most important fact for parents: Primary and Lower Secondary Checkpoint use exactly the same scoring methodology and six-band system. From May 2023 onward, both use the 0–50 scale and Rasch analysis. This means the grading language and structure your child encounters on the Primary result will be identical to the Lower Secondary structure they will see in Stages 7, 8, or 9.

However, the content is not the same. A score of 35 at Primary reflects mastery of Stage 6 curriculum (one year of Cambridge Primary material). A score of 35 at Lower Secondary reflects mastery of significantly more advanced material across one, two, or three stages. The score of 35 means "High" band in both cases, but the underlying achievement is different because the expectations for each stage differ.

Think of it this way: if your child scores in the High band at Primary Checkpoint, they are on track for Lower Secondary. That said, the Higher Secondary result at Stage 9 will be the primary readiness indicator for IGCSE — Primary is a useful checkpoint on the way, but Stage 9 is the main diagnostic bridge into Upper Secondary.

What subjects are tested, and how Global Perspectives works differently

Primary Checkpoint assesses four subjects. Three are traditional externally examined papers: English (available in first or second language streams), Mathematics, and Science. Each generates a 0–50 scaled score and performance band, reported in the same format as Lower Secondary Checkpoint.

Global Perspectives is structured differently. It is not a written exam marked by Cambridge. Instead, students work in teams to research and present a response to a real-world issue or question. Teachers at the school mark this project using Cambridge's assessment framework, and then Cambridge International externally moderates the marks to ensure consistency. The result still appears on the Checkpoint report, but it reflects a different assessment method — school-based project work plus Cambridge moderation, rather than an external paper mark conversion.

The broader picturePrimary Checkpoint tests breadth across four subjects at the end of Stage 6. If your child is preparing for the transition to Lower Secondary, this is the moment to identify any strand-level gaps (such as specific topics in Maths or Science where revision would help). The report should include strand-level subscores to guide that focus.
