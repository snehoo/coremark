How to Supervise a Child Who Uses an AI Tutor

Sit beside your child for the first session, check Coach's working, watch for copying, and keep sessions to 20 to 30 minutes.

CoreMark makes CoreMark Coach, so read this guide with that in mind. Where we point to other companies' pages, we link them directly.

Most parents who worry about a child using an AI tutor are not really worried about the technology. They want to know whether their child is learning or just collecting answers. This guide covers what to do in the first session, how to check an answer quickly, and what to look for over the following weeks.

It is written for parents of 12 to 14 year olds using CoreMark Coach for Cambridge Lower Secondary Stage 9. Nothing here replaces watching your own child work, and the steps are a starting point you can adjust.

What happens in the first session, minute by minute?

The first session sets the pattern for the rest, so plan on about 30 minutes. The steps below use only what Coach does by design, and they assume you have followed the CoreMark Coach setup.

  1. Sit beside your child, not across the room, with the chat open where you can both read it.
  2. Open the chat inside the Coach project and type "hi". Read Coach's greeting together. It introduces itself and asks the child to say "learn" and a topic, or "exam" and a topic.
  3. Let your child pick the topic. Coach teaches your child's own stage, so ask what they are studying this week in Maths, Science or Computing and let them name it.
  4. Watch the first learn loop. Coach opens with a map of the sub-concepts, teaches one at a time, draws a diagram first when it can, then gives an everyday example and the rule in exam wording.
  5. Watch the first teach-back. Coach asks your child to explain the idea in their own words. Let them try before you help. If the explanation is fuzzy, Coach re-teaches with a different example.
  6. Look at one apply question together and ask your child to read Coach's working out loud.
  7. At the end of the topic, Coach gives a three-question mini-check with no marks and suggests an exam on the topic, ideally next session. Write the topic on paper so you both know what to return to.

How do you check an answer in three minutes?

You do not need to check every answer. Pick one answer from the session and run the checklist below, which takes about three minutes.

  • Ask Coach to show its working for that answer, one step at a time.
  • Compare the final answer with the solutions in the matching PDF booster for the same topic, if you have one.
  • Ask your child to state the rule behind the first step without looking at the screen.
  • Check that the answer has the units or words the question asks for.
  • After a practice paper, read the first wrong step Coach names and ask your child to fix that one step.

What are the signs a child is copying rather than thinking?

Copying usually shows up as answers that arrive too quickly, too neatly, or in Coach's exact words. Treat these signs as clues that start a conversation, not as proof. If your child is struggling in the subject itself, the guide to what works when a child is falling behind in Cambridge Maths covers the gap underneath.

How long should a session last, and should you reuse a chat?

CoreMark's own guidance for Coach is twenty to thirty minutes most days, which beats one long session. Research on spacing is related, though it does not test session length directly. A 2006 synthesis of 317 experiments found that the best gap between study sessions grows with how long the material has to be remembered, although those studies used verbal recall tasks rather than maths or science.

Start a fresh chat for each session. Shorter chats are easier to look back through. Coach is designed to use Claude's memory to pick up between chats, but memory is a Claude feature, so it cannot be guaranteed to save everything. If Coach seems to have lost track, tell it where you left off.

⚠️
Breaks If your child is still going after thirty minutes, suggest a break before the next topic. A tired child is usually guessing.

What do you do when Coach says something that looks wrong?

Ask Coach to show its working before you decide anything. A confident tone does not mean an answer is right, and AI can make mistakes, so checking is part of using Coach.

Start with a calm message such as "Show me the working for that step." Coach re-derives each answer independently before it marks a student wrong. If its own question was flawed, it voids the question, replaces it and says so. If the working still looks wrong, compare the answer with a booster solution or your child's school textbook, and bring the question back in the next session. If you are not confident in the science yourself, the Checkpoint science help guide for non-expert parents explains what to look for.

Teach your child to say what they think is wrong and why. That habit is useful in the exam room as well as at the kitchen table.

What is Coach built not to do?

CoreMark Coach has guardrails written into every Coach file. It sticks to schoolwork for its own stage, teaches only its own stage and politely redirects requests for other stages. It uses a first name or nickname only and does not ask for personal details beyond what tutoring needs. The only photos it asks for are of your child's written work or drawings.

These are written instructions to an AI tutor, not a promise about every chat, so keep the screen visible and read what Coach writes now and then. Coach also only works inside Claude and is not free.
