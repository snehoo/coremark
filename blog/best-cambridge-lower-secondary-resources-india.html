Quick answer

For Cambridge Lower Secondary students in India, the best combination is: Cambridge official curriculum frameworks and specimen papers (free, from CAIE) for understanding what is tested and mock-paper practice, plus CoreMark topic boosters (₹249 per topic) for targeted revision of specific weak areas. Platforms like Byju's and Vedantu do not cover Cambridge Lower Secondary meaningfully — their Cambridge content targets IGCSE and above.

Why Cambridge Lower Secondary resources are scarce in India

India has a large and well-developed ed-tech sector, but it is built primarily around three curriculum systems: CBSE, ICSE, and Cambridge IGCSE. Cambridge Lower Secondary — the programme for students in Stages 7, 8, and 9, sitting the Checkpoint assessment — sits in a gap between these markets.

The schools that offer Cambridge Lower Secondary in India tend to be international schools in major cities: Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi, Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai. The student population is smaller than the CBSE and IGCSE markets, which means commercial ed-tech platforms have invested less in covering this curriculum specifically.

The result is that many Indian parents find themselves searching for resources and discovering that most of what appears in search results is either CBSE-aligned, IGCSE-pitched, or general-concept content that does not follow Cambridge Lower Secondary's specific strand structure, command words, or mark-scheme conventions.

Every option, honestly assessed

Cambridge official resources (free)

Cambridge Assessment International Education publishes free curriculum frameworks for all three Lower Secondary subjects: Mathematics (0862), Science (0893), and Computing (0860). These are downloadable PDFs that list every learning objective by stage and strand. They are the most authoritative free resource available and should be the first thing every parent reads before buying anything else.

Cambridge also publishes free specimen assessment materials — practice papers in the Checkpoint format — and a small number of past papers. The specimen papers are the closest thing to an official mock test. Their limitation is quantity: there are only a handful of official papers per subject, and most Stage 9 students will have used them by mid-year.

CoreMark is built specifically for Cambridge Lower Secondary Stages 7, 8 and 9. Each booster covers one topic — one cheat sheet, 20 levelled questions, and full worked solutions — written against the Cambridge curriculum frameworks. The 88 topics in the catalogue cover Maths (0862), Science (0893), and Computing (0860) across all three stages.

It is not a substitute for full-paper simulation — it is the targeted practice layer between learning a topic and attempting a past paper.

Hodder Education and Collins textbooks

Hodder Education is the official Cambridge-endorsed publisher for Lower Secondary textbooks. The Hodder Cambridge Lower Secondary Maths, Science, and Computing textbooks are comprehensive and well-aligned to the framework. They are available on Amazon India, typically in the ₹2,000–4,000 range per title.

The limitation for Indian families is cost and availability. A full set of Stage 8 textbooks across three subjects can cost ₹8,000–12,000, and delivery times from Amazon India can be slow for some titles. Schools that use Hodder books typically provide them — if your school does not, buying individual topic boosters for weak areas is often more economical than buying full textbooks.

Khan Academy (free, partial coverage)

Khan Academy covers most of the Maths concepts in the Cambridge Lower Secondary framework, and a good portion of Science. It is free, well-produced, and genuinely useful for conceptual explanation. What it does not provide is Cambridge-style question practice — its exercises use a different format, different mark conventions, and a different difficulty progression from Cambridge Checkpoint assessments.

Khan Academy is useful for understanding a concept. It is not reliable for Cambridge exam preparation specifically, because the question formats and command words are different.

Indian ed-tech platforms (Byju's, Vedantu, Unacademy)

These platforms are strong for what they are designed for: CBSE, ICSE, and competitive exam preparation. For Cambridge IGCSE, Vedantu offers some content. For Cambridge Lower Secondary specifically, coverage is minimal or nonexistent.

Parents who subscribe to these platforms for a Cambridge Lower Secondary student typically find that the content is misaligned — covering topics at the wrong depth, using different terminology, or pitching questions at an IGCSE level rather than a Lower Secondary Checkpoint level.

The most cost-effective combination

Recommended stack

Layer 1 — Free: Download the Cambridge curriculum frameworks for your child's subjects and stages from the CAIE website. Read through the learning objectives. Download specimen assessment materials as mock papers.

Layer 2 — Targeted practice: When a specific weak topic is identified (by school test results, specimen paper performance, or tutor feedback), buy the relevant CoreMark booster for that topic. At ₹249 per topic, you pay for exactly what is needed rather than a broad subscription.

Layer 3 — Mock papers: Use Cambridge specimen papers and any available past papers for full-paper simulation under timed conditions. Do this after targeted topic revision, not instead of it.

Total cost for a Stage 8 student revising four weak topics: ₹996 (4 CoreMark boosters), plus free official materials. This compares to ₹5,000–25,000/year for platforms that largely miss the Cambridge Lower Secondary curriculum.

India-specific buying notes

CoreMark boosters are PDF downloads — they arrive by email immediately after purchase and can be downloaded on any device. Printing at home on A4 works well; most urban Indian families have access to a home printer or a local print shop. Each booster is designed to print as a saddle-stitched booklet (two A4 sheets, eight pages) or simply as loose sheets.

Frequently asked questions

Byju's is built primarily for Indian board exams (CBSE, ICSE) and competitive entrance exams (JEE, NEET). It has some IGCSE content through its international offerings, but it does not have Cambridge Lower Secondary content aligned to the 0862, 0893, or 0860 frameworks. For a Stage 7, 8, or 9 Cambridge student, Byju's is not the right tool — the content will be misaligned to what is actually tested in Cambridge Checkpoint assessments.

Cambridge publishes specimen assessment materials (practice papers in the Checkpoint format) free on their website under the relevant syllabus page — search for "Cambridge Lower Secondary Checkpoint specimen papers" on cie.org.uk. Past papers from previous years are available through Cambridge's School Support Hub, which schools subscribe to. Some past papers also circulate through Cambridge-curriculum schools in India — ask your child's school coordinator. Third-party sites may host older papers but verify their alignment to the current framework.

Vedantu offers Cambridge International content, but their Cambridge coverage is primarily IGCSE and A Level. Cambridge Lower Secondary Maths (syllabus 0862, Stages 7–9) is not a core part of their catalogue. Some individual tutors on Vedantu may have experience with the Cambridge Lower Secondary framework, but the platform's structured content and test series are not built for Checkpoint preparation.

Cambridge Lower Secondary (Stages 7–9, typically ages 11–14) is the programme that precedes IGCSE. It is assessed by the Cambridge Checkpoint examination, scored on a 0–6 scale per subject strand rather than a letter grade. IGCSE (typically ages 14–16) is a different qualification with different syllabus codes, different assessment conventions, and a different grading system. Resources built for IGCSE are not reliable preparation for Cambridge Lower Secondary Checkpoint, and vice versa.
