Cambridge Checkpoint score calculator

Enter the score your child got in each subject (0 to 50). You'll see the band, what it means, what to do next, and how far it is to the next band. Leave any subject blank.

Use the 0–50 scaled scores from the Checkpoint statement of results. If your report shows a decimal like 4.2, it's from the old 0–6 scale Cambridge retired in 2023; see the grading guide.

The six Checkpoint bands

Every Checkpoint subject is reported on the same 0–50 scale, in six bands. A score of 21 or above (Good) means your child is working at the expected standard for the end of Lower Secondary.

Band thresholds are the same for every stage and subject, but the content behind them gets harder each stage. For how the 0–50 score is built from raw marks, see the full grading guide.

The subject score is only half the story

The Checkpoint report also breaks each subject into strands. A child in the Good band overall can still be weak in one strand. In Maths the content strands are Number, Algebra, Geometry and Measure, and Statistics and Probability. In Science, look at Biology, Chemistry and Physics separately. Fixing the weakest strand is usually the fastest way up a band.

Questions parents ask

What is a good Cambridge Checkpoint score?

Cambridge reports each subject on a 0–50 scale in six bands. A score of 21–30 (Good) or above means your child is working at the expected standard for the end of Lower Secondary; 31–40 is High and 41–50 is Outstanding.

Is Cambridge Checkpoint pass or fail?

No. Checkpoint is diagnostic. There is no pass mark, and a Basic or Unclassified result cannot be used to hold a student back. Schools use the strand feedback to plan teaching.

What does Unclassified mean on a Checkpoint result?

Unclassified (a score of 0) means no result could be reported for that subject or strand. It is not the same as a Basic result; ask the school for the specific reason.

Which subjects have a Checkpoint test?

English (or English as a Second Language), Mathematics and Science, plus Global Perspectives in the May and October series. Computing has no Checkpoint test.
